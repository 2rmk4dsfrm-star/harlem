Colour

Twelve colours, each with a job.

These are the exact values sampled from the poster. Lime always means money or emphasis. Sky always means "this is how you get in touch". If a colour has no job on the page, leave it out.

Graphic language

Everything leans forward.

The pay banner, the road and the background panels all share one 15° lean, so the design reads as movement. Keep to that one angle and don't add others.

£75–£200 per dayPaid at 80p per parcel
The 15° lean. Slant the trailing edge of banners and panels at 15° from vertical. In CSS, use skewX(-15deg) or a clip-path.

Background panel. One Route Navy panel leaning in from the top right, behind the headline or the van. One per layout.

Van and speed lines. A white van with a lime stripe, red tail-light and sky headlight, on a Lane Blue road with a sky dashed line. The speed lines are rounded bars in lime, sky and steel, with ragged left ends and aligned right ends.

You need a car or SWB van and hire & reward insurance.

Hi-vis underline. A 4 px lime underline marks the one requirement people must not miss. Use it once per piece.

Icons. 24 px line icons with a 1.8 px stroke and rounded ends, in lime on a 12 px-radius navy tile. Never fill them, and never use emoji in their place.

Voice

Say the number. Say it plainly.

Drivers read the poster in a few seconds, often on a phone. Lead with the pay, the place and the next step, and write the way people talk at the depot.

Numbers first

£200, 80p, Mon–Sat, Leeds. Facts drivers can check beat adjectives.

Short lines

Aim for one idea per line and fewer than ten words per line.

Straight about the deal

Say "self-employed", give the pay range as well as the top figure, and say what drivers need before they start.

One next step

Every piece ends with WhatsApp and the number. Don't offer competing calls to action.
